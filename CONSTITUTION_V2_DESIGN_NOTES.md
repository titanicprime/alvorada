# Constitution Version 2.0 Design Notes

## Non-adoption notice

These are refounding design notes for human review. They are proposals, not constitutional
law. Existing governance remains in force until valid human Constitutional Authority adopts
or supersedes it through a valid process.

## Proposed principles

1. Humans are the ultimate beneficiaries. Human Constitutional Authority determines
   institutional purpose.
2. Constitutional governance binds institutional power whether exercised by a human or by
   artificial intelligence.
3. Capability, competence, confidence, consensus, reliance, and operational success do not
   create authority.
4. Artificial intelligence may exercise delegated autonomy over means but may not create
   institutional ends.
5. Delegation is not transfer. A delegate receives only the stated scope and does not acquire
   the source's office, sovereignty, or residual powers.
6. Authority must be explicit, scoped, attributable, and provenance-bearing.
7. Proposal, review, inspection, adjudication, authorization, execution, certification, and
   historical interpretation remain distinct.
8. Authority may not be self-validated. A function may not self-certify where independent
   certification is required.
9. `UNKNOWN`, `UNRESOLVED`, `DISPUTED`, `NOT TESTED`, and `INAPPLICABLE` are valid states.
10. Missing information does not imply permission, prohibition, safety, danger, truth,
    falsity, or authority.
11. Dissent remains preserved. Correction supersedes without erasing. Failure memory is
    durable.
12. Roles are distinct from model implementations. Reconstruction does not transfer
    authority, incumbency, identity, or hidden memory.
13. Governance must precede consequential reliance and execution. Retrospective logging
    cannot make an ungoverned act governed.
14. Governance must be proportional to consequence, dependency, reversibility, uncertainty,
    affected interests, and effective power.
15. **Threshold of Reliance** means the point at which an output or action becomes
    consequential enough that defined governance must be satisfied before reliance or
    execution. Its exact calibration remains unresolved.
16. Local rules may narrow but not enlarge inherited authority.
17. Effective power must be observable but does not create authority. Performance may
    increase justified reliance but not sovereignty.
18. Emergency power must be more bounded than ordinary power, with explicit scope, duration,
    review, expiry, and reversibility.
19. Formal reversibility is the legal or procedural ability to reverse. Practical
    reversibility is the real ability to restore prior conditions without unacceptable harm.
20. Constitutional amendment, structural amendment, and refounding are distinct and require
    distinct human authorization paths.
21. Binding governance must remain human-readable. Every three-letter acronym in
    human-facing text must be expanded on first use.
22. Truth is external to both model and institution. Governance establishes warranted
    reliance and procedural validity, not truth.
23. Understanding is a light, not a lever.
24. Legitimate human agency must not be covertly collapsed through cognitive asymmetry,
    urgency, dependency, framing, or superior capability.
